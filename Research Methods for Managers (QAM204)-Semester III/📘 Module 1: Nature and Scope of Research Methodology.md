## **1.1.1 Definition & Objectives of Research**

### ✅ **Definition of Research:**

**Technical explanation:**
Research is a **systematic(পদ্ধতিগত / সিস্টেম্যাটিক)** and **scientific(বৈজ্ঞানিক / সায়েন্টিফিক)** process of Collecting (সংগ্রহ করা / কলোক্তিং),Analyzing (বিশ্লেষণ করা / অ্যানালাইজিং),Interpreting (ব্যাখ্যা করা / ইন্টারপ্রেটিং) information to answer a question or solve a problem.

**Layman’s explanation:**
Research means **finding out something new** in an organized way. Just like when you don't know the answer to a question and you search it on Google, in business we also try to find answers — like "Why are sales going down?" or "What do customers want?"

👉 **Real-life example:**
Imagine you have a small food delivery business. You notice people are ordering less. So, you start asking your old customers why they stopped ordering. You collect their answers, make a list of the common reasons, and plan changes to get them back. That’s research!

---

### ✅ **Objectives of Research (Why do we do research?):**

1. **To gain familiarity(পরিচিতি / ফ্যামিলিয়ারিটি)** with a Phenomenon (ঘটনা / ফেনোমেনন) or problem.
   🔹 *Layman: To understand something new.*
   🔹 *Example: A new company wants to know how people shop online in rural areas.*

2. **To describe(বর্ণনা করা / ডিস্ক্রাইব) things accurately.**
   🔹 *Layman: To give the correct picture of something.*
   🔹 *Example: A survey that shows 60% of college students prefer online classes.*

3. **To diagnose(সমস্যা চিহ্নিত করা / ডায়াগনোজ) the frequency(ঘনত্ব / ফ্রিকোয়েন্সি)** of an event or association between variables.
   🔹 *Layman: To find how often something happens and whether two things are related.*
   🔹 *Example: Does late-night screen time cause lower student performance?*

4. **To test a hypothesis(ধারণা / হাইপোথেসিস) or theory(তত্ত্ব / থিওরি).**
   🔹 *Layman: To check if an idea is true or false.*
   🔹 *Example: "If I give discounts, will sales increase?" — You test this idea by offering discounts and tracking the result.*

5. **To find solutions to problems (সমস্যার সমাধান).**
   🔹 *Layman: To fix what’s not working.*
   🔹 *Example: Research to reduce employee turnover in a company.*

## **1.1.2 Characteristics of Research**

Let’s break down the **main features (বৈশিষ্ট্য / ফিচারস)** of good research:

1. **Systematic process (পর্যায়ক্রমিক / সিস্টেম্যাটিক):**
   🔹 Research is done step by step – not randomly.
   🔹 *Example: You first define the problem, then collect data, analyze, and conclude.*

2. **Objective(নিরপেক্ষ / অবজেকটিভ):**
   🔹 It is based on facts, not opinions or feelings.
   🔹 *Example: Saying “Students prefer online classes” based on survey results, not just your own opinion.*

3. **Replicable(পুনরাবৃত্তিযোগ্য / রেপ্লিক্যাবল):**
   🔹 Others should be able to do the same research and get similar results.
   🔹 *Example: If someone else uses your method, they should find similar customer trends.*

4. **Empirical(অভিজ্ঞতালব্ধ / এম্পিরিক্যাল):**
   🔹 Based on real-world observations or data, not imagination.
   🔹 *Example: Sales reports, customer interviews, and surveys — these are empirical sources.*

5. **Logical(যুক্তিসঙ্গত / লজিক্যাল):**
   🔹 Follows rules of reasoning; conclusions should make sense.
   🔹 *Example: If more people like spicy food, and your spicy dish sells more — it’s logical.*

6. **Accuracy(সঠিকতা / অ্যাকিউরেসি):**
   🔹 The results should be correct and reliable.
   🔹 *Example: Your survey must be free from calculation mistakes or biased(পক্ষপাতদুষ্ট / বায়াসড) questions.*

## 📘 **1.1.3 Difference between Research Methods and Research Methodology**

### ✅ **Technical Explanation:**

* **Research Methods** are the specific **tools (সরঞ্জাম / টুলস)** and **techniques (পদ্ধতি / টেকনিক্স)** used to conduct research — like Surveys (সার্ভে / সার্ভে), Interviews (সাক্ষাৎকার / ইন্টারভিউ), Experiments (পরীক্ষা / এক্সপেরিমেন্ট), Observations (পর্যবেক্ষণ / অবজারভেশন), etc((এট সেতেরা/ ইত্যাদি).

* **Research Methodology** is the **overall approach (পন্থা / অ্যাপ্রোচ)** — the Logic and Reasoning Behind (যুক্তি এবং কারণ / লজিক অ্যান্ড রিজনিং বিহাইন্ড)
 choosing a certain method. It explains *why* and *how* a method is selected and used.

> **Shortcut:**
>
> * **Methods = What you do**
> * **Methodology = Why and how you do it**

---

### ✅ **Layman Explanation:**

Let’s say you’re baking a cake 🍰.

* The **method** is the actual steps: mixing flour, sugar, eggs, baking it at 180°C.
* The **methodology** is the reason you chose this recipe — why you baked it at 180°C and not 250°C, why you used eggs and not butter.

Same way in research:

* **Methods** = the tools you use (like online survey, interview, Google Forms).
* **Methodology** = your explanation of *why* you chose online survey and not face-to-face interviews.

---

### 🔍 **Real-Life Example (Business Research)**

You want to understand why your online clothing store has fewer sales this month.

* Your **method** is an **online survey (অনলাইন জরিপ / সার্ভে)** asking customers about their shopping habits.
* Your **methodology** is the explanation that:

  > “I chose an online survey because most of my customers are young and tech-savvy(প্রযুক্তিবান্ধব / টেক-স্যাভি), and I needed quick, low-cost feedback.”

## 📘 **1.2 Research Process**

### ✅ **Technical Explanation:**

The **research process** is a step-by-step (ধাপে ধাপে / স্টেপ বাই স্টেপ) journey followed to conduct research properly.

### ✅ **Layman Explanation:**

Imagine you're a detective 🕵️ trying to solve a mystery — let’s say, "Why did my best-selling product suddenly stop selling?"

You follow these steps (this is your **research process**):

---

### 🔢 **Steps in the Research Process:**

1. **Identifying the problem (সমস্যা চিহ্নিতকরণ / আইডেন্টিফাইং):**
   🔹 *"Why are my sales low this month?"*

2. **Reviewing literature (পূর্বের গবেষণা পড়া / রিভিউ লিটারেচার):**
   🔹 *Check what others have found about sales drops in similar businesses.*

3. **Setting objectives or hypothesis(ধারণা / হাইপোথেসিস):**
   🔹 *"I think customers are not buying because delivery charges increased."*

4. **Choosing the research method (পদ্ধতি নির্বাচন / মেথড):**
   🔹 *Online survey, in-person interview, or checking past sales data.*

5. **Collecting data (তথ্য সংগ্রহ / কালেক্টিং ডাটা):**
   🔹 *Ask customers through forms or record sales figures.*

6. **Analyzing data (ডেটা বিশ্লেষণ / অ্যানালাইজিং):**
   🔹 *Look at the answers and numbers to see trends.*

7. **Interpreting results (ফলাফল ব্যাখ্যা করা / ইন্টারপ্রেটিং):**
   🔹 *“Most customers stopped ordering when delivery fee increased.”*

8. **Reporting findings (রিপোর্ট তৈরি করা / রিপোর্টিং):**
   🔹 *Write a short report or presentation to explain what you learned and how to fix the issue.*

---

### 🔍 **Real-Life Business Example:**

A restaurant owner notices fewer online orders.

👉 Research Process:

1. Problem: Why fewer online orders?
2. Hypothesis: Maybe people don’t know about the new app.
3. Method: Online feedback form.
4. Data: Collected from 100 customers.
5. Analysis: 70% didn’t know the app existed.
6. Action: Launch app promotions.

## 📘 **1.2.1 Research Criteria and Research Process**

### ✅ **Technical Explanation:**

**Research Criteria** means the **standard (মানদণ্ড / স্ট্যান্ডার্ড)** or **quality checkpoints** that make research valid and useful. These are things that make research **reliable (নির্ভরযোগ্য / রিলায়েবল)** and **acceptable (গ্রহণযোগ্য / অ্যাকসেপ্টেবল)**.

### 📌 **Common Research Criteria:**

1. **Objectivity (নিরপেক্ষতা / অবজেকটিভিটি):** Free from personal opinion or bias.
2. **Reliability (নির্ভরযোগ্যতা / রিলায়াবিলিটি):** Same results if repeated again.
3. **Validity (প্রযোজ্যতা / ভ্যালিডিটি):** Research measures what it’s supposed to measure.
4. **Generalizability (সাধারণীকরণযোগ্যতা / জেনারেলাইজেবিলিটি):** Can the results apply to other groups or situations?
5. **Ethical (নৈতিক / এথিক্যাল):** Should not harm or cheat participants.

---

### ✅ **Layman Explanation:**

Let’s say you’re testing which color of T-shirt sells best.

🔸 If your survey asks only your 3 best friends — that’s **not objective (নিরপেক্ষ / অবজেকটিভ)**.
🔸 If your results change every time — it’s **not reliable (নির্ভরযোগ্য / রিলায়েবল)**.
🔸 If your survey asks about food instead of clothes — it’s **not valid (ভ্যালিড / প্রযোজ্য না)**.
🔸 If the results can be useful for other clothing businesses too — it’s **generalizable (জেনারেলাইজেবল / প্রযোজ্য অন্যত্রও)**.

## 📘 **1.2.2 Different Approaches of Research**

Let’s break these down in simple + technical language:

---

### 🔹 **Deductive Approach (নির্গমন পদ্ধতি / ডিডাকটিভ)**

✅ **Technical**: Starts from a **theory (তত্ত্ব / থিওরি)** → builds a **hypothesis (ধারণা / হাইপোথেসিস)** → tests it with data.

✅ **Layman**: You start with an idea and check if it’s true.

**Example**:
You believe “Customers buy more when prices drop.”
👉 So you reduce prices, then check sales data to confirm.
➡️ That’s **deductive**.

---

### 🔹 **Inductive Approach (আবরণ পদ্ধতি / ইনডাকটিভ)**

✅ **Technical**: Starts from **data (তথ্য / ডেটা)** → observes patterns → builds a theory.

✅ **Layman**: You observe first, then guess the rule.

**Example**:
You notice more orders on weekends.
👉 You don’t know why yet, but based on data, you make a theory: “People shop more when they’re free.”
➡️ That’s **inductive**.

---

### 🔹 **Quantitative Research (পরিমাণগত গবেষণা / কোয়ানটিটেটিভ)**

✅ **Technical**: Based on **numbers (সংখ্যা / নাম্বারস)**, statistics, and measurable data.

✅ **Layman**: Anything you can **count or measure**.

**Example**:
Survey with 100 people:

* “How many times did you shop last month?”
  ➡️ You collect numerical answers.

📊 **Tools**: Surveys, polls, data charts

---

### 🔹 **Qualitative Research (গুণগত গবেষণা / কোয়ালিটেটিভ)**

✅ **Technical**: Based on **feelings, opinions, behavior**, and **non-numerical** data.

✅ **Layman**: Understanding the **why or how** behind things.

**Example**:
You interview 10 customers:

* “Why do you prefer Brand A over Brand B?”
  ➡️ You analyze their words, tone, reasons.

📝 **Tools**: Interviews, focus groups, case studies

---

## 📘 **1.3 Types of Research Design**

### ✅ **1.3.1 Pure & Applied Research**

Let’s understand the difference between **pure research** and **applied research**:

---

### 🔹 **Pure Research (শুদ্ধ গবেষণা / পিউর রিসার্চ)**

#### ✅ **Technical Explanation:**

**Pure research** is the kind of research where the goal is to **gain knowledge** and **understand** things without worrying about practical applications. It is curiosity-driven and doesn’t immediately solve problems.

#### ✅ **Layman Explanation:**

Imagine you are learning about **how stars form**. You don’t care about whether this knowledge can help you in everyday life right now, but you’re just **curious** to know how it works. That’s pure research.

#### 🎯 **Real-Life Example:**

Scientists studying how ants communicate with each other through chemicals (called **pheromones**). They aren’t doing this to solve a real-world problem; they’re just **curious** about the ants' behavior.

---

### 🔹 **Applied Research (প্রযোজ্য গবেষণা / অ্যাপ্লাইড রিসার্চ)**

#### ✅ **Technical Explanation:**

**Applied research** is focused on solving **specific, practical problems**. It uses the knowledge gained from pure research to **find solutions** to real-world issues.

#### ✅ **Layman Explanation:**

Now, imagine you are **trying to create a better smartphone battery** using the knowledge you gained about chemistry. You’re doing it for a **specific purpose**, like making the phone last longer. That’s applied research.

#### 🎯 **Real-Life Example:**

A **pharmaceutical company** creating a new medicine for **cancer**. They use **pure research** (knowledge about cells) but focus it on **solving a problem** like cancer.

---

## 📘 **1.3.2 Causal & Correlational Research**

### 🔹 **Causal Research (কারণগত গবেষণা / কজাল রিসার্চ)**

#### ✅ **Technical Explanation:**

**Causal research** (also called **explanatory research**) is focused on finding **cause-and-effect relationships** between two or more variables. In simple terms, it answers **"Does A cause B?"**.

#### ✅ **Layman Explanation:**

Think about a study that shows **eating junk food** causes **weight gain**. The cause is eating junk food, and the effect is gaining weight. Causal research helps you find out **why** something happens.

#### 🎯 **Real-Life Example:**

A researcher tests if **watching TV for long hours** causes **poor grades** in school. Here, **watching TV** is the cause, and **poor grades** is the effect.

> In causal research, you try to prove one thing **causes** another.

---

### 🔹 **Correlational Research (সম্বন্ধগত গবেষণা / করিলেশনাল রিসার্চ)**

#### ✅ **Technical Explanation:**

**Correlational research** examines the **relationship** between two or more variables, but **doesn’t prove causality**. It just shows if things **move together** or are linked in some way.

#### ✅ **Layman Explanation:**

Think of a study that shows **more people eat ice cream during summer**, and **more people get sunburns**. But it doesn’t mean **eating ice cream causes sunburn**. They are **related** because both happen in the summer, but **one doesn’t cause the other**.

#### 🎯 **Real-Life Example:**

Researchers find a **strong relationship** between **higher income** and **more vacations taken**. But just because you have a higher income, it doesn't mean you automatically take more vacations. They **correlate**, but **one doesn’t cause the other**.

| **Causal Research**        | Finds cause-and-effect relationships                            | Does watching TV cause poor grades?        | কারণগত গবেষণা: টিভি দেখার ফলে কি খারাপ গ্রেড আসবে?                |
| **Correlational Research** | Finds relationships between variables (but doesn’t prove cause) | Do more vacations relate to higher income? | সম্বন্ধগত গবেষণা: উচ্চ আয় নিয়ে কি বেশি ছুটি নেওয়া সম্পর্কিত?   |

## 📘 **1.3.3 Cross-sectional & Longitudinal Research Designs**

---

### 🔹 **Cross-sectional Research (ক্রস-সেকশনাল গবেষণা / ক্রস-সেকশনাল রিসার্চ)**

#### ✅ **Technical Explanation:**

**Cross-sectional research** looks at **data from a specific point in time**. It provides a snapshot of a phenomenon and helps compare different groups or situations at that moment.

#### ✅ **Layman Explanation:**

Imagine you take a **picture** of a group of people at one particular moment to see what their preferences are. You're not interested in what happens over time, just **right now**.

#### 🎯 **Real-Life Example:**

A researcher surveys students to find out how many are **using smartphones** at one point in time. He doesn’t care about whether they start or stop using them later; it’s just a **snapshot** of their behavior at that **moment**.

---

### 🔹 **Longitudinal Research (দীর্ঘকালীন গবেষণা / লংজিটিউডিনাল রিসার্চ)**

#### ✅ **Technical Explanation:**

**Longitudinal research** follows the same group of people or data over a **long period of time**. It looks at how things change or develop over time.

#### ✅ **Layman Explanation:**

Imagine you’re studying how children’s **heights change** every year from age 5 to 10. You’re **observing over time**, not just at one point, so you can understand how things **evolve**.

#### 🎯 **Real-Life Example:**

A study that tracks a group of people from their childhood into adulthood to understand how their **eating habits** affect their health over time.

---

### 🔍 **Comparison Table (Cross-sectional vs Longitudinal):**

| Type                | Focus                           | Time Span                  | Example                                              |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **Cross-sectional** | A snapshot at one point in time | Single moment              | Survey of students on smartphone usage today         |
| **Longitudinal**    | Follows subjects over time      | Long period (months/years) | Tracking children’s eating habits over several years |

---

## 📘 **1.3.4 Experimental, Semi-experimental & Non-experimental Research**

---

### 🔹 **Experimental Research (বিশেষ পরীক্ষামূলক গবেষণা / এক্সপেরিমেন্টাল রিসার্চ)**

#### ✅ **Technical Explanation:**

In **experimental research**, researchers **manipulate** one or more variables to observe their effect on another variable, often in a **controlled setting**.

#### ✅ **Layman Explanation:**

Think of it like a **science experiment** where you change one thing (like the **temperature**) and observe how it affects something else (like **ice melting faster**).

#### 🎯 **Real-Life Example:**

You want to see if **drinking coffee** makes people more **alert**. You give one group coffee and another group no coffee, then measure their alertness after 30 minutes. You’re **controlling** the coffee factor to see its **effect**.

---

### 🔹 **Semi-experimental Research (অর্ধ-পরীক্ষামূলক গবেষণা / সেমি-এক্সপেরিমেন্টাল রিসার্চ)**

#### ✅ **Technical Explanation:**

**Semi-experimental research** involves manipulating some variables like in an experiment, but **lacks full control** over all aspects. For example, participants might already be part of a natural group (like a class) rather than being randomly assigned.

#### ✅ **Layman Explanation:**

You want to test if **exercise improves mood**, but you can’t randomly assign people to **exercise** or **no exercise**. Instead, you observe people who naturally already exercise vs those who don’t, and compare their moods. You can’t control everything, but you still see patterns.

#### 🎯 **Real-Life Example:**

A study looks at two groups of employees: one with a **regular exercise routine** and the other without. The researcher compares **mood** differences but can’t control how much exercise each person does.

---

### 🔹 **Non-experimental Research (অপরীক্ষামূলক গবেষণা / নন-এক্সপেরিমেন্টাল রিসার্চ)**

#### ✅ **Technical Explanation:**

**Non-experimental research** does not involve manipulating variables. It simply observes and gathers data without changing anything. It cannot prove cause-and-effect relationships, but it can show associations.

#### ✅ **Layman Explanation:**

Think of this as simply **watching** or **observing** how people behave without trying to change anything.

#### 🎯 **Real-Life Example:**

You conduct a study to see if there’s a **relationship** between **income** and **happiness**, but you’re not changing anyone’s income. You’re just **observing** and **measuring**.

---

### 🔍 **Comparison Table (Experimental, Semi-experimental & Non-experimental):**

| Type                  | Manipulation of Variables          | Control Over Variables | Example                                                |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Experimental**      | Yes, full control                  | High control           | Testing if coffee improves alertness                   |
| **Semi-experimental** | Some manipulation, limited control | Some control           | Observing employees with and without exercise habits   |
| **Non-experimental**  | No manipulation                    | No control             | Studying the relationship between income and happiness |

---

## 📘 **1.3.5 Descriptive & Exploratory Research**

---

### 🔹 **Descriptive Research (বর্ণনামূলক গবেষণা / ডেসক্রিপটিভ রিসার্চ)**

#### ✅ **Technical Explanation:**

**Descriptive research** focuses on **describing** characteristics or behaviors. It doesn’t explain why something happens, just **what happens**.

#### ✅ **Layman Explanation:**

This type of research is like taking a **detailed photo** of a situation, showing what’s happening without trying to figure out the cause.

#### 🎯 **Real-Life Example:**

A survey that asks people how often they eat out in a week. It simply **describes** the frequency of eating out in your community, without analyzing why people do it.

---

### 🔹 **Exploratory Research (অন্বেষণমূলক গবেষণা / এক্সপ্লোরেটরি রিসার্চ)**

#### ✅ **Technical Explanation:**

**Exploratory research** is conducted to **explore** an issue or problem that is not well-understood. It is the **first step** before doing more detailed research and **helps to gather initial insights**.

#### ✅ **Layman Explanation:**

Imagine you don’t know much about a topic. **Exploratory research** is your **first look** at the issue. It's like going on a **fact-finding mission** to learn more about the problem.

#### 🎯 **Real-Life Example:**

You’re interested in why students **don’t participate** in class. You start by doing some **preliminary interviews** or focus groups to understand why they don’t speak up. You’re **exploring** the issue.

---

### 🔍 **Comparison Table (Descriptive vs Exploratory):**

| Type            | Purpose                      | Approach                      | Example                                                       |
| --------------- | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Descriptive** | To describe what’s happening | Detailed, factual information | Survey on how often people eat out                            |
| **Exploratory** | To explore unknown issues    | Initial insights, open-ended  | Interviews to explore why students don’t participate in class |

## 📘 **1.4 Planning a Research Project**

---

### 🔹 **1.4.1 Identifying and Defining a Problem (সমস্যা চিহ্নিতকরণ ও সংজ্ঞায়িতকরণ / আইডেন্টিফাইং অ্যান্ড ডিফাইনিং আ প্রোবলেম)**

#### ✅ **Technical Explanation:**

In research, the **first step** is to **identify** a **problem** (issue or question) that needs to be addressed. Once identified, the **problem is defined** clearly so that the research can be focused on solving or answering it.

#### ✅ **Layman Explanation:**

Think about when you notice something **wrong** or **unanswered** in your everyday life. For example, you might **wonder** why people prefer **coffee** over tea in the mornings. The first step in research is **figuring out** what exactly you're trying to understand.

#### 🎯 **Real-Life Example:**

Imagine you’re a **coffee shop owner**. You **identify** that your **customers prefer** coffee more than tea, but you don’t know **why**. Your **problem** is to understand **why** coffee is preferred over tea.

---

### 🔹 **1.4.2 Research Problem Aspects & Considerations (গবেষণা সমস্যা দৃষ্টিভঙ্গি ও বিবেচনা / রিসার্চ প্রোবলেম অ্যাসপেক্টস অ্যান্ড কনসিডারেশন্স)**

#### ✅ **Technical Explanation:**

Once the problem is identified, you need to think about its **aspects** and **considerations**. This means breaking down the problem into smaller **parts** and thinking about all the factors that could affect it. **Considerations** include the **scope** of the research, its **feasibility** (how possible it is), and how it can impact the **solution**.

#### ✅ **Layman Explanation:**

Think of it like preparing for an event. You know you want to **organize a party** (research problem), but there are **many factors** you need to think about, like **budget**, **location**, **guests**, and **food**. All these aspects need to be considered to make the party **successful** (finding the solution to the research problem).

#### 🎯 **Real-Life Example:**

If you're investigating why people prefer **coffee over tea**, your considerations could include:

* **Age group** (Do younger people like coffee more than older people?)
* **Time of day** (Do people prefer coffee in the morning and tea in the evening?)
* **Costs** (Is coffee more affordable than tea in some areas?)

---

### 🔹 **1.4.3 Formulation of a Research Problem (গবেষণা সমস্যা রূপায়ণ / ফরমুলেশন অব আ রিসার্চ প্রোবলেম)**

#### ✅ **Technical Explanation:**

The **formulation of a research problem** means turning your identified problem into a **clear, researchable question** or hypothesis that you can test or explore. A well-formulated problem gives your research a **direction** and purpose. It should be **specific**, **clear**, and **researchable**.

#### ✅ **Layman Explanation:**

Formulating a research problem is like turning your **question** into something **clear** and **actionable**. For example, instead of asking, "Why do people like coffee?", you can turn it into a more **specific question** like, "How does drinking coffee in the morning affect people's productivity during work?"

#### 🎯 **Real-Life Example:**

Your original problem is that people **prefer coffee over tea**. You **formulate** the problem into a specific question:

* "What factors influence the preference of coffee over tea in the morning among office workers?"
  This is now a **researchable question** because you can gather data on office workers and analyze the factors that affect their choice.

---

### 🔍 **Summary of Key Concepts**

| Step                                          | What It Means                                            | Example                                                        |
| --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Identifying the Problem**                   | Finding the issue or question to explore                 | Why do people prefer coffee over tea in the morning?           |
| **Research Problem Aspects & Considerations** | Breaking the problem into smaller factors to think about | Age group, time of day, and costs involved in coffee vs tea    |
| **Formulation of the Problem**                | Creating a clear, researchable question or hypothesis    | "How does drinking coffee in the morning affect productivity?" |

---

## **Key Points to Remember:**

1. **Identify the Problem**: What do you want to understand or solve?
2. **Consider Aspects**: Think about all the factors that affect your problem (e.g., time, costs, demographics).
3. **Formulate a Clear Question**: Make your problem specific and easy to research.

## 🔍 What is **Empirical Research**?

### ✅ **Technical Explanation:**

**Empirical research** means research that is based on **observation (পর্যবেক্ষণ/অবজারভেশন)**, **experience (অভিজ্ঞতা/এক্সপেরিয়েন্স)**, or **experiments (পরীক্ষা/এক্সপেরিমেন্ট)**. It relies on **real data**, not just ideas or theories.

📌 Example of empirical research:

* Surveying 1,000 people to know how many prefer online shopping.

---

### ✅ **Layman Explanation:**

Empirical research is like finding out the truth by **seeing it for yourself** or **collecting real-life data**.
Imagine you want to know if students prefer **online classes** or **offline classes**. Instead of guessing, you ask 100 students and analyze their answers. That’s empirical research — **real data, real people**.

---

## ⚠️ Why is **Empirical Research Difficult in India**?

Here are some problems researchers often face in India:

---

### 1️⃣ **Lack of Reliable Data (বিশ্বস্ত ডেটা নেই / রিলায়াবল ডেটা)**

In India, sometimes it's hard to find **accurate (সঠিক/অ্যাকিউরেট)** or **updated information**.
📌 Example: You may not find proper income records of rural households.

---

### 2️⃣ **Low Awareness among Participants (অংশগ্রহণকারীদের কম সচেতনতা / লো অ্যাওয়ারনেস)**

Many people, especially in rural areas, don’t understand what research is and may not **cooperate (সহযোগিতা/কোপারেট)** or may give **false information**.
📌 Example: Someone may say what they think you want to hear, not what they actually feel.

---

### 3️⃣ **Language Barriers (ভাষার সমস্যা / ল্যাঙ্গুয়েজ ব্যারিয়ার)**

India is a multilingual country. A researcher from Kolkata may not understand Tamil or Marathi, which affects communication with respondents.

---

### 4️⃣ **Financial Constraints (অর্থের সীমাবদ্ধতা / ফিনান্সিয়াল কনস্ট্রেইন্ট)**

Empirical research needs **money** for things like printing surveys, traveling, paying data collectors, etc. In India, budget may be very limited.

---

### 5️⃣ **Poor Infrastructure (নিম্নমানের পরিকাঠামো / ইনফ্রাস্ট্রাকচার)**

Bad roads, lack of internet, or unreliable electricity can make it hard to collect data in remote areas.

---

## 📘 4.1 **Design of the Research Project**

**Meaning:** The research design is like a **blueprint (নকশা/ব্লুপ্রিন্ট)** or a **plan** for how you will do the research.

🔹 **Layman Explanation:**
Just like when you build a house, you first plan what materials, how many floors, rooms etc.—same way, in research, you decide:

* What is the topic?
* What data do you need?
* How will you collect it?
* From whom will you collect it?

📌 **Real-life Example:**
If you’re researching "Customer satisfaction of Domino’s Pizza," your design will say:

* Survey 100 customers
* Use Google Forms
* Analyze ratings using Excel

🟢 Bengali:
গবেষণার পরিকল্পনা মানে হলো পুরো রিসার্চ করার জন্য একটা সুন্দর প্ল্যান বা নকশা (Blueprint/ব্লুপ্রিন্ট) তৈরি করা।

---

## 📘 4.2 **Ex Post Facto Research**

**Meaning:** “Ex post facto” (লাতিন শব্দ) means **after the fact (ঘটনার পর / অ্যাফটার দ্য ফ্যাক্ট)**. This is a type of research where you study something **after it already happened**—you don’t control the situation.

🔹 **Layman Explanation:**
You’re not doing any experiment. You just observe something that **already happened** and study its reasons.

📌 **Example:**
You’re studying whether watching violent TV shows in childhood leads to aggression in adulthood. You **can't change** their past—you just collect and analyze the facts.

🟢 Bengali:
ঘটনার পরে যেটা ঘটে গেছে, সেটা নিয়ে রিসার্চ করা মানেই Ex Post Facto Research (এক্স পোস্ট ফ্যাক্টো রিসার্চ)। আপনি অতীতের ঘটনা নিয়ে কাজ করছেন।

---

## 📘 4.3 **Motivation in Research**

**Meaning:** Motivation (উৎসাহ/মোটিভেশন) means the **reason or desire** behind doing research.

🔹 **Layman Explanation:**
Why do people do research? What drives them? That is motivation.

📌 **Example Reasons for Motivation:**

* Solve a real-life problem
* Get a degree (like BBA project)
* Win awards or funding
* Find the truth
* Help society

🟢 Bengali:
গবেষণা করার পিছনে যেই আগ্রহ বা উৎসাহ থাকে, সেটাই হলো Motivation (মোটিভেশন)। যেমন: ভালো গ্রেড পাওয়া, কোনো সমস্যার সমাধান খোঁজা ইত্যাদি।

---

## 📘 4.4 **Objectives of Research**

**Meaning:** What is the **goal or purpose (উদ্দেশ্য/পারপাস)** of your research?

🔹 **Layman Explanation:**
What do you want to find out by doing this research?

📌 **Types of Objectives:**

* **Descriptive (বর্ণনামূলক/ডেসক্রিপটিভ)** – Describe something (e.g. buying behavior of college students)
* **Exploratory (অনুসন্ধানমূলক/এক্সপ্লোরেটরি)** – Explore something unknown (e.g. new trends in marketing)
* **Causal (কারণ নির্ধারণকারী/কজাল)** – Find cause-effect (e.g. Does price affect demand?)

🟢 Bengali:
আপনার গবেষণার উদ্দেশ্য (Objective/অবজেক্টিভ) মানে আপনি ঠিক কী জানতে বা প্রমাণ করতে চাচ্ছেন।

## 📘 4.5 **Criteria of Good Research**

**Meaning:** What makes research **good, reliable (বিশ্বস্ত/রিলায়াবল)** and **useful (উপযোগী/ইউজফুল)?**

🔹 **Layman Explanation:**
A good research should have:

1. **Clarity (স্বচ্ছতা/ক্ল্যারিটি)** – Clear goals
2. **Accuracy (সঠিকতা/অ্যাকিউরেসি)** – No errors
3. **Objectivity (নিরপেক্ষতা/অবজেকটিভিটি)** – No personal bias
4. **Validity (বৈধতা/ভ্যালিডিটি)** – Data must be relevant
5. **Reliability (নির্ভরযোগ্যতা/রিলায়াবিলিটি)** – Should give same results if repeated

📌 **Example:**
If your research on student stress gives the same result when someone else does it again—that’s reliable research.

🟢 Bengali:
ভালো গবেষণার জন্য দরকার হলো স্পষ্ট উদ্দেশ্য, নির্ভুল তথ্য, নিরপেক্ষতা, এবং ফলাফলের নির্ভরযোগ্যতা।

## 📘 4.6 **Research and Scientific Method**

**Meaning:** The **scientific method (বৈজ্ঞানিক পদ্ধতি/সায়েন্টিফিক মেথড)** is a step-by-step way to **find the truth using evidence (প্রমাণ/এভিডেন্স)**.

🔹 **Steps (Technically):**

1. Define the problem
2. Review literature
3. Form a hypothesis
4. Collect data
5. Analyze data
6. Draw conclusion

🔹 **Layman Explanation:**
It’s like being a detective. You first see the problem, then guess the reason (hypothesis), then collect proof, and finally find the truth.

📌 **Example:**
You think “Students who exercise regularly have better concentration.”
→ You test this by surveying students and checking their grades.
→ That’s scientific research.

🟢 Bengali:
গবেষণায় যখন আপনি ধাপে ধাপে, যুক্তিসম্মত ও প্রমাণভিত্তিক (Evidence-based) পদ্ধতিতে কাজ করেন, তখনই সেটা হয় Scientific Method (সায়েন্টিফিক মেথড)।

### 🔍 Easy Explanation of Key Types:

---

### **1. Pure vs Applied Research**

* **Pure (মৌলিক/পিওর):** Just to gain knowledge.
  🧪 *Example:* Why does the sky look blue?
  No immediate use, but adds knowledge.

* **Applied (প্রয়োগমূলক/অ্যাপ্লাইড):** Solves real-life problems.
  📈 *Example:* Best way to market a new soft drink.

---

### **2. Quantitative vs Qualitative**

* **Quantitative (সংখ্যাগত/কোয়ান্টিটেটিভ):** Deals with numbers.
  📊 *Example:* 60% of customers are happy with Domino's.

* **Qualitative (গুণগত/কোয়ালিটেটিভ):** Deals with feelings or reasons.
  🎤 *Example:* Why do some customers feel Domino’s is slow?

---

### **3. Descriptive vs Exploratory**

* **Descriptive (বর্ণনামূলক/ডেসক্রিপটিভ):** Describes what is happening.
  📋 *Example:* What age group orders most pizza?

* **Exploratory (অনুসন্ধানমূলক/এক্সপ্লোরেটরি):** Explores unknown areas.
  🔍 *Example:* Why are fewer people visiting malls?

---

### **4. Causal vs Correlational**

* **Causal (কারণ নির্ধারণ/কজাল):** One thing causes another.
  🧪 *Example:* Does TV ad increase sales?

* **Correlational (সম্পর্কমূলক/করেলেশনাল):** Just shows connection, not cause.
  🔗 *Example:* Are people who exercise happier?

---

### **5. Cross-sectional vs Longitudinal**

* **Cross-sectional (এককালীন/ক্রস-সেকশনাল):** One-time study.
  🕒 *Example:* Survey of students’ opinion this month.

* **Longitudinal (দীর্ঘমেয়াদী/লংগিটিউডিনাল):** Study over time.
  📅 *Example:* Track student stress for 2 years.

### 🔶 **What is Research Process?**

Research process মানে হলো—আপনি ধাপে ধাপে (Step by Step/স্টেপ বাই স্টেপ) কিভাবে একটা রিসার্চ করবেন।

It’s like a recipe (রান্নার রেসিপি)—you must follow steps to get the right outcome.

---

### 📊 **Flowchart of Research Process:**

```
1. Identify Problem
       ↓
2. Review Existing Research (Literature)
       ↓
3. Set Objectives & Hypothesis
       ↓
4. Choose Research Design
       ↓
5. Data Collection
       ↓
6. Data Analysis
       ↓
7. Interpretation & Conclusion
       ↓
8. Report Writing & Presentation
```

#### **1. Identify the Research Problem (সমস্যা চিহ্নিত করা / আইডেন্টিফাই)**

🔹 What are you researching?
🔹 Example: "Why are students stressed before exams?"

📘 Bengali:
প্রথমে ঠিক করুন আপনি কোন সমস্যাটা নিয়ে গবেষণা করতে যাচ্ছেন।

📘 Bengali:
এই বিষয়ে আগে কে কী রিসার্চ করেছে, সেটা দেখে নিন।

📘 Bengali:
আপনার রিসার্চ কী উদ্দেশ্যে করছেন ও আপনি কী ধারণা করছেন সেটা লিখুন।

#### **4. Choose Research Design (গবেষণার পদ্ধতি ঠিক করা / ডিজাইন)**

📘 Bengali:
আপনি কোন পদ্ধতিতে তথ্য সংগ্রহ করবেন তা ঠিক করুন।

📘 Bengali:
আপনার পাওয়া ফলাফলগুলো ব্যাখ্যা করুন এবং উপসংহার টানুন।

#### **8. Report Writing & Presentation (রিপোর্ট লেখা ও উপস্থাপন)**

### 🔶 **What is Social Research?**

**Social Research (সামাজিক গবেষণা/সোশ্যাল রিসার্চ)** studies how people behave in society.

🔹 Example topics: Poverty, Unemployment, Education, Crime, Gender issues.

---

### 📌 **Uses of Social Research (Layman + Bengali):**

---

### 1️⃣ **Policy Making (নীতি নির্ধারণ / পলিসি মেকিং)**

🔹 Governments use social research to make policies.

📘 Example: If research shows high unemployment, the govt may launch a job scheme.

📘 Bengali:
সরকার নতুন নিয়ম বা স্কিম তৈরি করতে সামাজিক গবেষণা ব্যবহার করে।

---

### 2️⃣ **Solving Social Problems (সমস্যা সমাধান)**

🔹 Understand issues like drug addiction, crime, or mental health.

📘 Bengali:
মানুষের সমস্যা বোঝা যায় এবং তা সমাধানে সাহায্য হয়।

---

### 3️⃣ **Understanding Behavior (আচরণ বোঝা / বিহেভিয়ার)**

🔹 How do teenagers behave on social media? Why do people protest?

📘 Bengali:
মানুষের আচরণ ও চিন্তা-ভাবনা বোঝার জন্য গবেষণা দরকার।

---

### 4️⃣ **Evaluating Programs (প্রকল্প মূল্যায়ন / ইভ্যালুয়েশন)**

🔹 Is a govt program (like free food) actually helping people?

📘 Bengali:
কোনো সরকারি স্কিম কতটা সফল হয়েছে, সেটা জানার জন্য।

---

### 5️⃣ **Business & Marketing Research (বিজনেস ও মার্কেট বোঝা)**

🔹 Understand customer habits, social trends to plan advertising.

📘 Bengali:
লোকজনের পছন্দ-অপছন্দ বুঝে প্রোডাক্ট তৈরি বা বিজ্ঞাপন করা।
